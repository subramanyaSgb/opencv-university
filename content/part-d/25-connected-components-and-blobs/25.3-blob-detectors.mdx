# 25.3 Blob detectors (LoG, MSER)

<Definition term="Blob detector">a method that finds compact regions that are brighter or darker than their surroundings, directly in a grey image: their centre, size and sometimes shape. Unlike threshold-and-label (25.1), blob detectors look at local contrast and can separate blobs that a single threshold would merge or miss.</Definition>

Labelling needs a good binary mask. On uneven backgrounds, with blobs of different brightness, or with blobs that touch, a single threshold fails. Three families of detectors avoid it: the scale-normalised Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG, 20.4) finds blob centres and sizes from second derivatives; OpenCV's SimpleBlobDetector thresholds at many levels and keeps regions that persist; MSER (Matas et al., 2002 [2]) finds regions whose outline stays stable while the threshold changes.

## 1. An analogy

Finding hills on a map. LoG looks for places where the ground curves down in every direction, and the curvature tells how wide the hill is. SimpleBlobDetector floods the map to many water levels and keeps the islands that appear at several levels. MSER floods slowly and keeps the islands whose coastline barely moves while the water rises: those are the clearly shaped hills.

The LoG treats each dome as a separate maximum of curvature, so touching particles are counted separately. Its result depends on the peak threshold (here a wide range from about 30 to 60 works) and on the scale range, which must cover the blob sizes. SimpleBlobDetector is convenient but its default settings and filters must be adapted; MSER produces many nested regions per blob that must be merged.

## 3. SimpleBlobDetector in detail

`cv2.SimpleBlobDetector` thresholds the image from `minThreshold` to `maxThreshold` in steps of `thresholdStep`, finds contours at each level, groups centres closer than `minDistBetweenBlobs`, keeps groups seen at least `minRepeatability` times, and filters by:

- **colour** (`blobColor` 0 = dark blobs, 255 = bright blobs);
- **area** (`minArea`, `maxArea`);
- **circularity** 4πA/P² (24.3), **inertia ratio** (minor/major axis from moments, 24.6), **convexity** (area / hull area, 24.5).

Defaults (OpenCV 4.13): thresholds 50 to 220 in steps of 10, area 25–5 000, inertia ≥ 0.1 and convexity ≥ 0.95 on, circularity off, `blobColor = 0`. The keypoints it returns have `pt` (centre) and `size` (diameter).

## 4. Industrial example

Blob detection finds solder-paste deposits, ball-grid-array balls, holes in perforated plates, bubbles in glass and cells in microscope images. LoG-type detectors are chosen when blobs vary in brightness or touch; SimpleBlobDetector is popular for quick setups (calibration-target dots, LED arrays) because its filters encode "round, convex, of this size" directly; MSER is used for text and markings, whose characters are stable regions under thresholding, and as a feature detector for matching (34).

## Code

Two examples: SimpleBlobDetector and MSER, then a LoG scale-space detector.

### 1. SimpleBlobDetector and MSER

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

img = cv2.imread("sample-particles.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
p = cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_Params()
print("default blobColor:", p.blobColor, "-> blobs:", len(cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_create(p).detect(img)))
p.blobColor = 255                                                    # bright blobs
kp = cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_create(p).detect(img)
print("bright blobs:", len(kp), "| diameters", round(min(k.size for k in kp), 1), "to", round(max(k.size for k in kp), 1))
p.filterByInertia = p.filterByConvexity = False
print("without shape filters:", len(cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_create(p).detect(img)))

mser = cv2.MSER_create(delta=5, min_area=80, max_area=800)
regions, boxes = mser.detectRegions(img)
centres = []
for r in regions:                                                    # merge nested regions of the same blob
    c = r.mean(axis=0)
    if all(np.hypot(*(c - k)) > 6 for k in centres):
        centres.append(c)
print("MSER regions:", len(regions), "| distinct centres:", len(centres))
```

Output:

```text
default blobColor: 0 -> blobs: 0
bright blobs: 31 | diameters 11.0 to 24.1
without shape filters: 35
MSER regions: 472 | distinct centres: 39
```

### 2. LoG scale-space detector

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

f = img.astype(np.float32)
sigmas = [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]
stack = np.stack([-cv2.Laplacian(cv2.GaussianBlur(f, (0, 0), s), cv2.CV_32F) * s * s for s in sigmas])  # bright blobs -> positive
best = stack.max(axis=0)
scale = np.array(sigmas)[stack.argmax(axis=0)]
for t in (30, 60, 90):
    peaks = (best == cv2.dilate(best, np.ones((9, 9), np.uint8))) & (best > t)
    print(f"threshold {t}: {int(peaks.sum())} blobs")
peaks = (best == cv2.dilate(best, np.ones((9, 9), np.uint8))) & (best > 30)
ys, xs = np.nonzero(peaks)
radii = np.sqrt(2) * scale[ys, xs]
print("estimated radius range:", round(float(radii.min()), 1), "to", round(float(radii.max()), 1), "px")
```

Output:

```text
threshold 30: 40 blobs
threshold 60: 39 blobs
threshold 90: 20 blobs
estimated radius range: 5.7 to 11.3 px
```

- The radius estimate r ≈ √2 · σ (20.4) recovers the particles' size range (6–13 px) to within the coarse σ steps.

## OpenCV API notes

- **`cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_create(params)`**, **`detector.detect(image, mask=None)`** → list of `cv2.KeyPoint` (`pt`, `size`). Parameters in `cv2.SimpleBlobDetector_Params()`; set `blobColor`, `minArea`/`maxArea`, the `filterBy*` switches and their limits, `minThreshold`/`maxThreshold`/`thresholdStep`, `minDistBetweenBlobs`.
- **`cv2.MSER_create(delta=5, min_area=60, max_area=14400, max_variation=0.25, min_diversity=0.2, …)`**, **`mser.detectRegions(gray)`** → (list of point arrays, bounding boxes). Detects dark regions on light and light on dark in one call (two passes).
- **LoG / DoG**: build from `GaussianBlur` and `Laplacian` (20.4); `cv2.SIFT_create` contains a DoG blob detector with sub-pixel and sub-scale refinement (34.1).
- **Drawing**: `cv2.drawKeypoints(img, kp, None, (0, 0, 255), cv2.DRAW_MATCHES_FLAGS_DRAW_RICH_KEYPOINTS)` draws circles of the keypoint size.

## Where it fails

- **Non-round objects**: LoG responds to elongated shapes with several maxima or weak ones.
- **Strongly overlapping blobs**: when centres are closer than about the radius, LoG maxima merge too.
- **Texture and noise** at the chosen scales: many false blobs; raise the threshold or restrict scales.
- **MSER on low-contrast or blurred blobs**: regions are not stable enough to be detected.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">

- **Forgetting `blobColor = 255`** for bright blobs.
- **Leaving the default shape filters on** when objects are not round and convex.
- **Counting raw MSER regions**: each blob yields several nested regions.
- **LoG without σ² normalisation**: small scales dominate.

</Callout>

## 🔍 Go deeper

<GoDeeper>

### Scale-space blob detection

Lindeberg [1] proposed detecting blobs as local maxima of the scale-normalised Laplacian (or the determinant of the Hessian) over space and scale, which gives both position and characteristic size. SIFT (34.1) and SURF use these detectors as keypoints.

### MSER

Matas, Chum, Urban and Pajdla [2] defined maximally stable extremal regions for wide-baseline stereo matching: regions that are connected components of a threshold level set and whose area changes least over a range of thresholds. They can be computed in almost linear time with a component tree (Nistér and Stewénius 2008), and they are invariant to monotonic brightness changes and affine transformations of the region.

</GoDeeper>

## Exercises

### Exercise 1 (by hand)

A SimpleBlobDetector with thresholds 50 to 220 in steps of 10 thresholds the image how many times? A blob appears in 5 of those binary images; with `minRepeatability = 2`, is it kept?

<Solution>

(220 − 50) / 10 = 17 steps, i.e. thresholds 50, 60, …, 210: **17** images. Seen 5 times ≥ 2: **kept**.

</Solution>

### Exercise 2 (coding)

Draw the LoG detections (threshold 30) as circles of radius √2 · σ on a colour copy of the particle image, and check how many detections lie inside the particle mask.

<Solution>

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

_, mask = cv2.threshold(img, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
vis = cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_GRAY2BGR)
inside = 0
for x, y, r in zip(xs, ys, radii):
    cv2.circle(vis, (int(x), int(y)), int(round(r)), (0, 0, 255), 1)
    inside += int(mask[y, x] > 0)
print("detections:", len(xs), "| inside the particle mask:", inside)
```

Output:

```text
detections: 40 | inside the particle mask: 40
```

All 40 detections sit on particles: there are no false detections on the background at this threshold. Comparing detections with the binary mask is a quick sanity check when no ground truth is available.

</Solution>
