# 24.1 Finding contours

<Definition term="Contour">the outline of a connected white region in a binary image, stored as an ordered list of boundary pixel coordinates that goes once around the region. Contours turn a mask (millions of pixels) into a few shapes (lists of points) that can be measured, compared and drawn.</Definition>

Edges (22) and masks (13, 23) are still images. Most inspection questions are about objects: how many, how big, which shape, where. `cv2.findContours` is the bridge: it follows the border of every white region and returns it as a polygon. Everything in this module (hierarchy, area, perimeter, bounding shapes, hulls, moments) starts from its output.

## 1. An analogy

Walking around a pond while keeping your left hand on the fence: you pass every post in order and return to where you started. Writing down the position of each post gives the pond's contour. A pond with an island has a second fence, around the island, which you walk separately.

## 2. Border following

OpenCV implements Suzuki and Abe's border-following algorithm (1985) [1]. It finds outer borders (around white regions) and hole borders (around black regions inside white ones), and records which border encloses which (24.2).

A worked example: a 4 × 4 white square at x = 2…5, y = 2…5. With `CHAIN_APPROX_NONE`, the contour lists all 12 boundary pixels in order: (2, 2), (2, 3), (2, 4), (2, 5), (3, 5), (4, 5), (5, 5), (5, 4), (5, 3), (5, 2), (4, 2), (3, 2). With `CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE`, straight horizontal, vertical and diagonal runs are compressed to their end points: (2, 2), (2, 5), (5, 5), (5, 2).

The contour runs through the **centres** of the boundary pixels. Its polygon area is 3 × 3 = **9**, although the square contains 16 pixels: area and perimeter computed from contours describe the outline through pixel centres, about half a pixel inside the true edge (24.3).

On the six parts of the test image (outer contours only):

| Part | Points (NONE) | Points (SIMPLE) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plate (rectangle) | 360 | **4** |
| L-bracket | 269 | **7** |
| Washer (disc) | 212 | 116 |
| Rotated bar | 224 | 212 |
| Star | 220 | 112 |
| Oval | 176 | 84 |

`SIMPLE` saves a lot on axis-aligned shapes (4 corners for the plate) and less on curved or rotated ones, where few runs are perfectly straight. Use `NONE` when you need every boundary pixel (curvature, sub-pixel fitting), `SIMPLE` otherwise.

## 3. The input must be a clean binary image

- **Objects white, background black**: findContours traces non-zero regions. Dark parts on a light background must be inverted first; otherwise the whole background becomes one big object (inverting the parts image and asking for external contours gives exactly one contour: the frame).
- **Masks, not edge maps**: running findContours on a Canny image traces both sides of every one-pixel edge line and every small edge fragment. On the parts image, Canny + `RETR_LIST` returns 21 contours instead of 10. Threshold or segment into filled regions first (13, 27); use edges only when you really want curves.
- **Clean first**: specks and holes become contours too (23.2).
- **Type**: 8-bit single channel (or 32-bit integer labels with `RETR_CCOMP`/`RETR_FLOODFILL`); float images raise an error.

## 4. Industrial example

A sorting station thresholds a back-lit image of parts on a conveyor and calls findContours on the mask. Each outer contour is one part: its area rejects fragments and touching pairs, its bounding box gives the pick position, and its shape decides the part type (24.3–24.6, 29). The whole step takes well under a millisecond per frame for a few dozen parts, because the contour lists are small compared with the image.

## Mini exercise

**Q1.** A white 10 × 10 square. How many points does its contour have with CHAIN_APPROX_NONE, and with SIMPLE?

<Solution>

NONE: the boundary pixels of a 10 × 10 square: 4 × 10 − 4 = **36**. SIMPLE: **4** (the corners).

**Q2.** Your parts are dark on a light conveyor. What happens if you call findContours on the thresholded image directly (parts = 0, conveyor = 255)?

<Solution>

</Solution>

## Code

Two examples: the small square, then the parts image with both approximations.

### 1. A contour by hand

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

square = np.zeros((8, 8), np.uint8)
square[2:6, 2:6] = 255                                               # 4 x 4 white pixels
none, _ = cv2.findContours(square, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
simple, _ = cv2.findContours(square, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE)
print("NONE:  ", none[0].reshape(-1, 2).tolist())
print("SIMPLE:", simple[0].reshape(-1, 2).tolist())
print("contour area:", cv2.contourArea(none[0]), "| white pixels:", int((square > 0).sum()))
```

Output:

```text
NONE:   [[2, 2], [2, 3], [2, 4], [2, 5], [3, 5], [4, 5], [5, 5], [5, 4], [5, 3], [5, 2], [4, 2], [3, 2]]
SIMPLE: [[2, 2], [2, 5], [5, 5], [5, 2]]
contour area: 9.0 | white pixels: 16
```

- Each contour is an array of shape (N, 1, 2), int32, with (x, y) points; `reshape(-1, 2)` makes it an N × 2 list.

### 2. The parts image

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

parts = cv2.imread("sample-parts.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
none, hierarchy = cv2.findContours(parts, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
simple, _ = cv2.findContours(parts, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE)
for c_none, c_simple in zip(none, simple):
    x, y, w, h = cv2.boundingRect(c_none)
    print(f"part at ({x:3d}, {y:3d}), {w:3d} x {h:3d}: NONE {len(c_none):3d} points | SIMPLE {len(c_simple):3d}")

edges = cv2.Canny(parts, 50, 150)
on_edges, _ = cv2.findContours(edges, cv2.RETR_LIST, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
on_mask, _ = cv2.findContours(parts, cv2.RETR_LIST, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
inverted, _ = cv2.findContours(255 - parts, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
print("contours on the mask:", len(on_mask), "| on Canny edges:", len(on_edges), "| external on the inverted mask:", len(inverted))

canvas = cv2.cvtColor(parts, cv2.COLOR_GRAY2BGR)
cv2.drawContours(canvas, none, -1, (0, 0, 255), 2)                    # -1 = all contours
```

Output:

```text
part at (228, 127),  75 x  47: NONE 176 points | SIMPLE  84
part at (147, 118),  56 x  65: NONE 220 points | SIMPLE 112
part at ( 22, 115),  96 x  70: NONE 224 points | SIMPLE 212
part at (115,  20),  61 x  76: NONE 269 points | SIMPLE   7
part at ( 17,  17),  77 x  77: NONE 212 points | SIMPLE 116
part at (200,  15), 101 x  81: NONE 360 points | SIMPLE   4
contours on the mask: 10 | on Canny edges: 21 | external on the inverted mask: 1
```

- The order of the contours is the order in which the scan meets them (bottom to top here); do not rely on it, sort by position if needed.

## OpenCV API notes

**`contours, hierarchy = cv2.findContours(image, mode, method, offset=(0, 0))`** (OpenCV 4: two return values; OpenCV 3 returned three):
- `image`: 8-bit single channel, non-zero = object. Not modified since OpenCV 3.2.
- `mode`: `cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL` (outer contours only), `RETR_LIST` (all, no hierarchy), `RETR_CCOMP` (two levels), `RETR_TREE` (full nesting) (24.2).
- `method`: `cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE`, `CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE`, `CHAIN_APPROX_TC89_L1`, `CHAIN_APPROX_TC89_KCOS` (Teh–Chin dominant points).
- Returns a tuple of (N, 1, 2) int32 arrays and a (1, K, 4) hierarchy array.

**`cv2.drawContours(image, contours, contourIdx, color, thickness)`**: `contourIdx=-1` draws all; `thickness=-1` (`cv2.FILLED`) fills, which turns contours back into masks.

**Related**: `cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats` (25.1) when you only need areas and boxes, not outlines.

## Where it fails

- **Touching objects**: one contour for the pair; separate them first (23.2, 23.5, 27.4).
- **Noisy masks**: many tiny contours; filter by area or clean the mask.
- **Objects cut by the image border**: their contour follows the border; flag contours touching it.
- **Precision**: pixel-centre polygons are biased by about half a pixel; use sub-pixel edges for measurement (22.5).

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">

- **Black objects on white background** without inverting.
- **Contours on Canny output** when filled regions were meant.
- **Unpacking three return values** (OpenCV 3 code) in OpenCV 4: `ValueError`.
- **Assuming a fixed contour order**.
- **Comparing contour area with pixel counts**: they differ by about half the perimeter.

</Callout>

## 🔍 Go deeper

<GoDeeper>

### Border following

Suzuki and Abe [1] scan the image in raster order; each time they meet an unvisited border pixel they follow the border with an 8-neighbour (for the object) search and label it with a new border number. The numbers of the borders crossed before reaching a pixel define the parent–child relation, which gives the hierarchy in one pass.

### Chain codes

The NONE output is equivalent to a Freeman chain code (29.1): a start point plus one of 8 directions per step. Chain codes compress contours, and their statistics (number of diagonal steps) give better perimeter estimates than the polygon length.

</GoDeeper>

## Exercises

### Exercise 1 (by hand)

A white shape: a 3 × 3 square at x = 0…2, y = 0…2, plus one extra pixel at (0, 3) below its bottom-left corner. List the corner points that CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE would keep.

<Solution>

Boundary walk: (0, 0) down to (0, 3), then up-right diagonal to (1, 2), right to (2, 2), up to (2, 0), left back to (0, 0). Corners kept: **(0, 0), (0, 3), (1, 2), (2, 2), (2, 0)**: five points (the diagonal step from (0, 3) to (1, 2) is a run of its own).

</Solution>

### Exercise 2 (coding)

Fill the contours of the parts image back into a mask with `drawContours(..., thickness=-1)` using `RETR_EXTERNAL`. How many pixels differ from the original mask, and why?

<Solution>

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

filled = np.zeros_like(parts)
cv2.drawContours(filled, none, -1, 255, thickness=cv2.FILLED)
diff = (filled > 0) != (parts > 0)
print("pixels that differ:", int(diff.sum()), "| original white:", int((parts > 0).sum()), "| refilled white:", int((filled > 0).sum()))
```

Output:

```text
pixels that differ: 2118 | original white: 20352 | refilled white: 22470
```

Filling only the external contours fills the holes too: the washer's hole, the plate's two holes (and the pin inside one of them) become white. Those 2 118 pixels are the hole areas (minus the pin, which was already white). To reproduce the mask with holes, draw with `RETR_CCOMP` or `RETR_TREE` and fill holes in black (24.2).

</Solution>
