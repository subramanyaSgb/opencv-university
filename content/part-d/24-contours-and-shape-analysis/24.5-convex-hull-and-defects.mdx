# 24.5 Convex hull and defects

<Definition term="Convex hull and convexity defects">the convex hull is the smallest convex polygon that contains a shape: a rubber band stretched around it. Wherever the shape curves inwards, there is a gap between the band and the outline: a convexity defect, described by where it starts and ends on the hull, its deepest point, and its depth.</Definition>

Many shape errors are dents: a notch punched in the wrong place, a chipped edge, a missing tooth, a bent tab. A convex part has no defects at all, so any defect deeper than the pixel noise is suspicious. Defects also count protrusions: the gaps between the fingers of a hand or the points of a star. The tools are `cv2.convexHull` and `cv2.convexityDefects`.

## 1. An analogy

Wrap a rubber band around a shape cut out of cardboard. On a disc or a rectangle the band touches the outline everywhere. On a star it jumps from point to point, leaving a triangular gap between every two points. The size of each gap, measured from the band to the deepest point of the outline, is the defect's depth.

## 3. Practical rules

- **Hull points vs indices**: `convexHull(c)` returns points for drawing and area; `convexHull(c, returnPoints=False)` returns indices, which `convexityDefects` needs.
- **Depth units**: the fourth value of each defect is the depth × 256 (fixed point); divide by 256.
- **Thresholds in pixels or relative**: a dent of 2 pixels matters on a 50-pixel part but not on a 1 000-pixel one; relative depth (depth / hull diameter) is often better.
- **Solidity** is a one-number convexity check: below about 0.95 for parts that should be convex means missing material or a deformation.
- **Hull for robust size**: the hull's perimeter and area ignore inward noise on the outline, which makes them more stable than the contour's.

## 4. Industrial example

Stamped and moulded parts that should be convex (washers, discs, rectangular plates, bottle caps) are checked for edge defects by solidity and by the deepest convexity defect: a chipped rim or a short shot in moulding leaves a dent in the outline. For parts with intended concavities (brackets, gears, combs), the expected number and depth of defects is part of the specification: a gear with a broken tooth has one defect too few between neighbouring teeth, or one that is too wide.

## Code

Two examples: hulls and solidity, then defects with a depth filter.

### 1. Hulls and solidity

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

parts = cv2.imread("sample-parts.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
contours, _ = cv2.findContours(parts, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)
names = ["oval", "star", "bar", "L-bracket", "washer", "plate"]
for name, c in zip(names, contours):
    hull = cv2.convexHull(c)                                          # hull points
    solidity = cv2.contourArea(c) / cv2.contourArea(hull)
    print(f"{name:9s} hull points {len(hull):2d} | solidity {solidity:.3f}")
```

Output:

```text
oval      hull points 35 | solidity 0.985
star      hull points  6 | solidity 0.516
bar       hull points 12 | solidity 0.970
L-bracket hull points  5 | solidity 0.677
washer    hull points 36 | solidity 0.986
plate     hull points  4 | solidity 1.000
```

### 2. Convexity defects

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

def defects(c, min_depth=2.0):
    idx = cv2.convexHull(c, returnPoints=False)                      # indices, required by convexityDefects
    d = cv2.convexityDefects(c, idx)
    if d is None:
        return []
    return [(tuple(c[s][0]), tuple(c[e][0]), tuple(c[f][0]), depth / 256) for s, e, f, depth in d[:, 0] if depth / 256 >= min_depth]

for name, c in zip(names, contours):
    found = defects(c)
    print(f"{name:9s} {len(found)} defect(s) deeper than 2 px" + (f": depths {[round(float(x[3]), 1) for x in found]}" if found else ""))
start, end, far, depth = defects(contours[3])[0]
print("L-bracket defect: start", [int(v) for v in start], "end", [int(v) for v in end], "farthest", [int(v) for v in far], f"depth {depth:.1f}")
```

Output:

```text
oval      0 defect(s) deeper than 2 px
star      6 defect(s) deeper than 2 px: depths [12.3, 12.5, 14.0, 13.4, 13.1, 13.0]
bar       0 defect(s) deeper than 2 px
L-bracket 1 defect(s) deeper than 2 px: depths [31.8]
washer    0 defect(s) deeper than 2 px
plate     0 defect(s) deeper than 2 px
L-bracket defect: start [135, 95] end [175, 40] farthest [135, 41] depth 31.8
```

## OpenCV API notes

- **`cv2.convexHull(points, clockwise=False, returnPoints=True)`**: Sklansky's algorithm, O(N log N); `returnPoints=False` returns indices into `points`.
- **`cv2.convexityDefects(contour, convexhull_indices)`** → (K, 1, 4) int32 array `[start, end, farthest, fixpt_depth]` or `None`; depth = fixpt_depth / 256. Requires indices, a contour with at least 4 points, and a hull that is monotonic (use the hull from the same contour).
- **`cv2.isContourConvex(contour)`**: True only for exactly convex polygons; digitised curves are rarely convex at pixel level, so apply it after `approxPolyDP`.

## Where it fails

- **Self-intersecting or noisy contours**: convexityDefects can raise "The convex hull indices are not monotonous"; simplify the contour or use `CHAIN_APPROX_NONE` with a cleaned mask.
- **Shallow, wide dents**: small depth even if large in area; also check the defect's area (the polygon between hull edge and contour).
- **Holes**: the hull of the outer contour ignores holes; a missing hole does not change solidity.
- **Defects on both sides of thin parts**: a thin bent wire has defects that describe its bend, not damage.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">

- **Passing hull points instead of indices** to `convexityDefects`.
- **Forgetting /256** on the depth.
- **No depth filter**: dozens of sub-pixel "defects" on every curved part.
- **Using isContourConvex on raw digitised contours** and concluding nothing is convex.

</Callout>

## 🔍 Go deeper

<GoDeeper>

### Hull algorithms

Graham's scan (1972) and Andrew's monotone chain sort the points and keep only left turns, O(N log N); for simple polygons such as contours, Melkman's algorithm builds the hull in linear time. OpenCV uses Sklansky's algorithm [1], which is linear for simple polygons when the points are ordered.

### Concavity trees

Recursively computing hulls of the defect regions gives a concavity tree: the shape, its concavities, the concavities of those concavities, and so on. It is a classic hierarchical shape descriptor for recognition and for describing complex outlines such as leaves or gears [2].

</GoDeeper>

### Exercise 2 (coding)

Count "fingers": draw a hand-like shape (a palm disc of radius 30 plus five rectangles of 12 × 40 pointing outwards at −60°, −30°, 0°, 30° and 60° from vertical), and count the deep defects. How many fingers does that imply?

<Solution>

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

hand = np.zeros((200, 200), np.uint8)
cv2.circle(hand, (100, 120), 30, 255, -1)
for a in (-60, -30, 0, 30, 60):
    box = cv2.boxPoints(((100 + 50 * np.sin(np.radians(a)), 120 - 50 * np.cos(np.radians(a))), (12, 40), a)).astype(np.int32)
    cv2.fillPoly(hand, [box], 255)
c = cv2.findContours(hand, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_NONE)[0][0]
print("all defects deeper than 10 px:", sorted(round(float(d[3]), 1) for d in defects(c, min_depth=10)))
deep = defects(c, min_depth=20)
print("deep defects:", len(deep), "-> fingers:", len(deep) + 1)
```

Output:

```text
all defects deeper than 10 px: [14.2, 14.6, 39.6, 40.1, 40.3, 40.3]
deep defects: 4 -> fingers: 5
```

Four deep valleys (about 40 px) lie between the five fingers. Two more defects of about 14 px sit between the outer fingers and the palm; a 20-pixel threshold separates the two groups. Choosing that threshold from the shape, not from one example, is the hard part of real finger counting. The "defects + 1" rule is the classic convexity-defect finger counter.

</Solution>
