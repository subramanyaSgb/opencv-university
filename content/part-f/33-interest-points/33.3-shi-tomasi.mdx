# 33.3 Shi–Tomasi

<Definition term="Shi–Tomasi corner (good feature to track)">a pixel whose structure tensor has a large smaller eigenvalue: score = min(λ1, λ2). Shi and Tomasi (1994) showed that this is exactly the condition for a window to be trackable reliably from frame to frame; OpenCV's goodFeaturesToTrack selects such points with a quality threshold and a minimum spacing.</Definition>

Harris (33.2) avoids eigenvalues with a clever formula. Shi and Tomasi asked a more direct question: "Will the tracker be able to follow this window?" The answer is "yes, if the smaller eigenvalue is large enough", so they use λ1 itself as the score.

## 1. An analogy

A chain is only as strong as its weakest link. A corner is only as well located as its weakest direction. Harris mixes the two directions into one number; Shi–Tomasi simply asks for the weakest one.

## 2. The score and the selection

The smaller eigenvalue in closed form, from M = [[A, B], [B, C]]:

```text
λmin = (A + C)/2 − √( ((A − C)/2)² + B² )
```

Measured (Sobel, 3 × 3 sums):

| Point | λmin | Harris R |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flat | 335 | 2.0 × 10⁵ |
| straight edge | 372 | −7.4 × 10⁹ |
| corner of the box | 103 640 | 2.7 × 10¹⁰ |
| checkerboard crossing | **693 220** | **4.4 × 10¹¹** |

Both scores agree on what is a corner. The difference is in borderline cases: λmin falls only linearly with the weaker direction, Harris's R also punishes a strong λ2 through −k·trace². **Curved edges** (the disc) have a moderate λmin, so with a low quality level Shi–Tomasi accepts them; Harris does not.

## 3. A worked example by hand

**Scores.** M = [[200, 100], [100, 200]]: (A + C)/2 = 200, √(0 + 100²) = 100 → λmin = **100** (and λmax = 300).

**Selection.** qualityLevel 0.5, minDistance 5, maxCorners 0 (all). Four local maxima:

| Point | Score | Above 0.5 × 9 = 4.5? | Distance to accepted | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 (10, 10) | 9 | yes | none yet | **accept** |
| P2 (14, 10) | 8 | yes | 4 to P1 | reject (< 5) |
| P3 (30, 10) | 7 | yes | 20 to P1 | **accept** |
| P4 (12, 30) | 5 | yes | √(4 + 400) ≈ 20.1 to P1 | **accept** |

The strongest point always wins; weaker points too close to it are dropped, even when they are good (P2 scores 8). With maxCorners = 2, only P1 and P3 remain.

## 4. On the feature scene

`cv2.goodFeaturesToTrack(img, maxCorners, qualityLevel, minDistance)`:

| maxCorners | qualityLevel | minDistance | Corners |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (all) | 0.01 | 10 | 61 |
| 0 | 0.01 | 1 | 103 |
| 0 | 0.05 | 10 | 48 |
| 25 | 0.01 | 10 | 25 |

The five strongest are all checkerboard crossings, where four high-contrast squares meet.

**Repeatability** between the two views (quality 0.01, within 2 px):

| Score | minDistance 1 | minDistance 5 | minDistance 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shi–Tomasi (λmin) | 78 of 101 (77 %) | 60 of 77 (78 %) | 36 of 59 (61 %) |
| Harris (k 0.04) | 57 of 59 (97 %) | 51 of 54 (94 %) | 36 of 48 (75 %) |

Two lessons. First, on this scene Shi–Tomasi picks 16 points along the disc's edge (minDistance 5); they slide along the curve between views and are not found again. Harris picks none there. Second, a large **minDistance lowers repeatability**: the greedy choice depends on which neighbour happens to be strongest, which can differ between views. minDistance is for spreading points, not for quality.

## 5. Which one to use

| Situation | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Tracking with optical flow (Lucas–Kanade, 40.3) | Shi–Tomasi: designed for it, the default in most trackers |
| Sharp man-made corners, curved edges in the scene | Harris, or Shi–Tomasi with a higher qualityLevel |
| Need N well-spread points | goodFeaturesToTrack with maxCorners and minDistance (either score) |
| Need matching across views with scale change | neither: use SIFT, ORB or AKAZE (Module 34) |

## 6. Industrial use

- **Point tracking** on moving belts, webs and vehicles: Shi–Tomasi + Lucas–Kanade optical flow (Module 40).
- **Video stabilisation** (39.7): track Shi–Tomasi corners, estimate camera motion, warp the frames.
- **Visual odometry** on AGVs (Module 43) with well-spread corners.

## Code

### 1. The smaller eigenvalue from scratch

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

img = cv2.imread("sample-feat.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
f = img.astype(np.float64)
gx = cv2.Sobel(f, cv2.CV_64F, 1, 0, ksize=3)
gy = cv2.Sobel(f, cv2.CV_64F, 0, 1, ksize=3)
S = lambda a: cv2.boxFilter(a, -1, (3, 3), normalize=False)
A, B, C = S(gx * gx), S(gx * gy), S(gy * gy)
lam_min = (A + C) / 2 - np.sqrt(((A - C) / 2) ** 2 + B ** 2)     # smaller eigenvalue, closed form
harris = A * C - B * B - 0.04 * (A + C) ** 2
pts = {"flat": (250, 120), "edge": (55, 20), "box corner": (20, 20), "checker X": (132, 122)}
for name, (x, y) in pts.items():
    print(f"{name:10s} min eigenvalue {lam_min[y, x]:10.0f}   Harris R {harris[y, x]:10.3e}")
ref = cv2.cornerMinEigenVal(img, 3, 3).astype(np.float64)
print("max relative difference to cv2.cornerMinEigenVal:", round(float(np.abs(lam_min / (4 * 3 * 255) ** 2 - ref).max() / ref.max()), 8))
```

Output:

```text
flat       min eigenvalue        335   Harris R  2.016e+05
edge       min eigenvalue        372   Harris R -7.397e+09
box corner min eigenvalue     103640   Harris R  2.724e+10
checker X  min eigenvalue     693220   Harris R  4.425e+11
max relative difference to cv2.cornerMinEigenVal: 1.8e-07
```

### 3. Repeatability: Shi–Tomasi vs Harris

```python
b = cv2.imread("sample-feat-b.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)
c, s = 0.9 * np.cos(np.radians(15)), 0.9 * np.sin(np.radians(15))
H = np.array([[c, -s, 40], [s, c, -25], [0.0002, 0.0001, 1.0]])
H_inv = np.linalg.inv(H)

def inside_b(p):
    """Inside view B with a 7 px margin, and its source in view A too."""
    if not (7 <= p[0] <= 312 and 7 <= p[1] <= 192):
        return False
    u = cv2.perspectiveTransform(p.reshape(1, 1, 2), H_inv).ravel()
    return 7 <= u[0] <= 312 and 7 <= u[1] <= 192

def repeatability(pa, pb, tol=2.0):
    mapped = cv2.perspectiveTransform(pa.reshape(-1, 1, 2), H).reshape(-1, 2)
    keep = [p for p in mapped if inside_b(p)]
    return int(sum(np.hypot(*(pb - p).T).min() <= tol for p in keep)), len(keep)

for use_harris in (False, True):
    for md in (1, 5, 10):
        detect = lambda im: cv2.goodFeaturesToTrack(im, 0, 0.01, md, useHarrisDetector=use_harris).reshape(-1, 2)
        hit, n = repeatability(detect(img), detect(b))
        print(f"{'Harris     ' if use_harris else 'Shi-Tomasi '} minDistance {md:2d}: {hit} of {n}")
```

Output:

```text
Shi-Tomasi  minDistance  1: 78 of 101
Shi-Tomasi  minDistance  5: 60 of 77
Shi-Tomasi  minDistance 10: 36 of 59
Harris      minDistance  1: 57 of 59
Harris      minDistance  5: 51 of 54
Harris      minDistance 10: 36 of 48
```

## OpenCV API notes

- **`cv2.goodFeaturesToTrack(image, maxCorners, qualityLevel, minDistance[, corners[, mask[, blockSize[, useHarrisDetector[, k]]]]])`**: 8-bit or float32 single channel. Returns float32 (N, 1, 2) of (x, y), strongest first, or `None` if nothing is found.
  - `maxCorners` ≤ 0: no limit. `qualityLevel`: fraction of the best score (0.01 typical). `minDistance`: Euclidean, in pixels.
  - `mask`: 8-bit, non-zero where corners may be found (e.g. a region of interest). `blockSize` default 3. `useHarrisDetector=False` (Shi–Tomasi) by default; `k` 0.04.
  - There is also an overload with `gradientSize` (the Sobel aperture, default 3).
- **`cv2.cornerMinEigenVal(src, blockSize, ksize=3)`**: the score map itself (float32), scaled like cornerHarris.
- **`cv2.GFTTDetector_create(...)`**: the same detector as a `cv2.Feature2D` object, returning `cv2.KeyPoint`s; it plugs into descriptor pipelines (Module 34).

## Where it fails

- Same scale limitation as Harris: one window size.
- **Curved edges and blobs** at low quality levels give points that slide.
- **qualityLevel is relative**: one very strong corner (a reflection, a checkerboard) raises the bar and removes weaker but useful corners elsewhere. Use a mask, or detect per image tile.
- **minDistance** is greedy and order-dependent: different views can keep different neighbours.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">

- **Not checking for `None`**: goodFeaturesToTrack returns `None` on a blank image; `len(None)` crashes.
- **Assuming integer coordinates**: the result is float32 (x, y); cast before indexing, and index as `img[y, x]`.
- **Using a huge minDistance for "quality"**: it only spreads points.
- **Detecting once and forgetting**: in tracking, re-detect when too many points are lost.

</Callout>

## 🔍 Go deeper

<GoDeeper>

### Why λmin means trackable

Shi and Tomasi [1] considered tracking a window by solving the linear system M·d = e for the displacement d (the Lucas–Kanade step, 40.3). The system is well conditioned only if both eigenvalues of M are well above the noise level; λmin is the bottleneck. They also proposed checking the window's dissimilarity over time under an affine model, to reject features that change appearance.

### Spreading features evenly

Greedy minDistance is simple but not optimal. Alternatives: detect per grid cell (as ORB-SLAM does with FAST), or adaptive non-maximal suppression (ANMS, Brown, Szeliski and Winder [2]), which keeps points that are the strongest within the largest radius and gives a much more even spread.

</GoDeeper>

## Exercises

### Exercise 1 (by hand)

Points with scores: P1 (0, 0) 10, P2 (3, 4) 9, P3 (8, 0) 6, P4 (8, 3) 5, P5 (20, 20) 0.8. qualityLevel 0.1, minDistance 5, maxCorners 0. Which points are returned, in which order?

<Solution>

Threshold 0.1 × 10 = 1: P5 is dropped. P1 accepted. P2 at distance 5 from P1: OpenCV rejects only distances **below** minDistance, so P2 is accepted. P3: 8 from P1, √(25 + 16) ≈ 6.4 from P2 → accepted. P4: 3 from P3 → rejected. Result: **P1, P2, P3**.

</Solution>

### Exercise 2 (coding)

Detect at most 30 Shi–Tomasi corners only in the lower half of the image (rows 100 and below), using a mask. How many are on the checkerboard (x 120–167, y 110–157)?

<Solution>

```python
mask = np.zeros_like(img)
mask[100:, :] = 255
c = cv2.goodFeaturesToTrack(img, 30, 0.01, 5, mask=mask).reshape(-1, 2)
on_board = ((c[:, 0] >= 120) & (c[:, 0] <= 167) & (c[:, 1] >= 110) & (c[:, 1] <= 157)).sum()
print(len(c), "corners,", int(on_board), "on the checkerboard, topmost row", int(c[:, 1].min()))
```

Output:

```text
30 corners, 23 on the checkerboard, topmost row 110
```

The checkerboard's crossings are the strongest points, so with maxCorners 30 they take most of the budget.

</Solution>

### Exercise 3 (thinking)

You track corners on a moving web. The only strong structure is a printed logo at the left edge, and goodFeaturesToTrack returns all points there. What do you change?

<Solution>

The relative threshold is dominated by the logo. Detect per tile (a grid of masks with a few corners each), or lower qualityLevel and use minDistance plus maxCorners per tile, so the tracked points cover the whole web.

</Solution>
