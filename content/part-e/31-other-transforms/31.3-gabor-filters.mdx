# 31.3 Gabor filters

<Definition term="Gabor filter">a filter whose kernel is a sinusoidal wave multiplied by a Gaussian window. It responds strongly where the image contains stripes of a particular direction and wavelength, near that position: a local, oriented frequency detector (Gabor 1946 [1]; Daugman 1985 [2]).</Definition>

Fourier analysis (30.x) finds frequencies but not their positions; wavelets (31.2) find positions but only in three fixed directions. A Gabor filter can be tuned to any direction and wavelength and is local. Banks of Gabor filters model the orientation-selective cells of the visual cortex and are a classic tool for texture: fabric, wood, paper, machined surfaces, fingerprints and iris codes.

## 1. An analogy

Running a comb over a fabric. A comb with the same tooth spacing as the threads, held in the threads' direction, slides into them and "rings"; held across them or with the wrong spacing it barely engages. A Gabor filter is a comb with a chosen spacing (λ) and angle (θ), and its response tells where the fabric matches.

## 2. The kernel

<Flow
  steps={[
    { label: "Rotate coordinates by θ: x′ = x cos θ + y sin θ, y′ = −x sin θ + y cos θ", tone: "data" },
    { label: "Gaussian window: exp(−(x′² + γ² y′²) / 2σ²)", tone: "process" },
    { label: "Carrier wave: cos(2π x′ / λ + ψ)", tone: "process" },
    { label: "Kernel = window × wave (cv2.getGaborKernel)", tone: "result" },
  ]}
/>

| Parameter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| θ | direction of the wave's variation; stripes in the image perpendicular to it respond (θ = 0: vertical stripes) |
| λ | wavelength in pixels: the stripe period it responds to |
| σ | size of the Gaussian window: how local the filter is (σ ≈ 0.5 λ is common) |
| γ | aspect ratio: γ < 1 makes the window longer along the stripes |
| ψ | phase: 0 gives an even (symmetric) kernel, π/2 an odd (antisymmetric) one |

## 3. A worked example by hand

Kernel values for θ = 0, λ = 8, σ = 4, γ = 0.5, ψ = 0 (window exponent −(x² + 0.25 y²) / 32):

| Position (x, y) | Window | Wave cos(2πx/8) | Kernel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (0, 0) | 1 | 1 | **1.000** |
| (4, 0) | e^(−16/32) = 0.607 | cos π = −1 | **−0.607** |
| (8, 0) | e^(−64/32) = 0.135 | cos 2π = 1 | **0.135** |
| (0, 4) | e^(−4/32) = 0.882 | 1 | **0.882** |

Along x the kernel alternates every half wavelength; along y (the stripe direction) it stays positive and fades slowly, because γ = 0.5 stretches the window. `cv2.getGaborKernel` returns exactly these values. Note that the kernel's sum is 2.14, not 0: an even Gabor kernel also responds to the mean brightness, so subtract the mean (or use the odd kernel, or pairs) before measuring texture.

## 4. Finding a texture defect

The fabric image has vertical and horizontal threads (period 8 px), a band where the vertical threads are missing, and a knot. Filter with four orientations (λ = 8, σ = 4, γ = 0.5) and average |response| over 15 × 15 windows (**local energy**):

| θ | Energy outside the band | Inside the band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0° (vertical threads) | 1 925 | **532** |
| 45° | 47 | 63 |
| 90° (horizontal threads) | 1 949 | 1 975 |
| 135° | 47 | 53 |

Only the 0° channel sees the defect: its energy drops to 28 % in the band, while the 90° channel is unchanged (the horizontal threads are intact). Thresholding the 0° energy at its mean − 4 standard deviations (measured outside the band) finds the band with an IoU of 0.79 (the 15 × 15 averaging rounds its corners). The 45° and 135° channels are nearly silent: the fabric has no diagonal structure.

## 5. Industrial use

- **Textile inspection**: missing threads, holes, slubs and weave faults as local energy drops or changes in a Gabor bank.
- **Surface texture**: grain direction in wood, grinding marks on metal, orientation of fibres.
- **Fingerprints**: Gabor filters tuned to the local ridge direction and spacing enhance ridges before minutiae extraction.
- **Iris recognition**: Daugman's iris codes are the signs of complex Gabor responses [2].
- **Texture features** for classification: mean and variance of each filter's energy (35.x).

## Code

Three examples: kernel values, an orientation bank, and defect detection.

### 1. Kernel values

```python
import numpy as np
import cv2

k = cv2.getGaborKernel((21, 21), 4.0, 0, 8.0, 0.5, 0, ktype=cv2.CV_64F)   # (size, sigma, theta, lambda, gamma, psi)
c = 10                                                                      # centre index
print("centre:", k[c, c], "| (4, 0):", round(k[c, c + 4], 3), "| (8, 0):", round(k[c, c + 8], 3), "| (0, 4):", round(k[c + 4, c], 3))
print("sum:", round(float(k.sum()), 3))
```

Output:

```text
centre: 1.0 | (4, 0): -0.607 | (8, 0): 0.135 | (0, 4): 0.882
sum: 2.142
```

- `theta` and `psi` are in **radians**; `lambd` is spelled without the a (lambda is a Python keyword).

### 2. An orientation bank

```python
img = cv2.imread("sample-fabric.png", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE).astype(np.float32)
y, x = np.mgrid[0:256, 0:256]
band = (x > 150) & (x < 172) & (y > 60) & (y < 200)                     # where the vertical threads are missing

energy = {}
for deg in (0, 45, 90, 135):
    k = cv2.getGaborKernel((21, 21), 4.0, np.deg2rad(deg), 8.0, 0.5, 0, ktype=cv2.CV_32F)
    response = cv2.filter2D(img - img.mean(), cv2.CV_32F, k)
    energy[deg] = cv2.blur(np.abs(response), (15, 15))                   # local energy
    print(f"theta {deg:3d}: outside {energy[deg][~band].mean():7.1f}, inside {energy[deg][band].mean():7.1f}")
```

Output:

```text
theta   0: outside  1924.8, inside   532.3
theta  45: outside    47.1, inside    63.0
theta  90: outside  1948.5, inside  1974.9
theta 135: outside    47.3, inside    52.7
```

### 3. Detecting the defect

```python
e = energy[0]
mu, sd = e[~band].mean(), e[~band].std()
defect = e < mu - 4 * sd
iou = (defect & band).sum() / (defect | band).sum()
n, labels, stats, _ = cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats(defect.astype(np.uint8))
print("threshold:", round(float(mu - 4 * sd), 1), "| defect pixels:", int(defect.sum()), "| IoU with the band:", round(float(iou), 3))
print("regions:", n - 1, "| largest box (x, y, w, h):", stats[1:, :4][np.argmax(stats[1:, 4])].tolist())
```

Output:

```text
threshold: 1358.9 | defect pixels: 3653 | IoU with the band: 0.794
regions: 2 | largest box (x, y, w, h): [147, 57, 27, 147]
```

- The largest region's box (x 147–173, y 57–203) matches the band (x 151–171, y 61–199) within the 15 × 15 energy window; the second, small region is a false alarm.

## OpenCV API notes

- **`cv2.getGaborKernel(ksize, sigma, theta, lambd, gamma, psi=CV_PI*0.5, ktype=CV_64F)`**: note the default ψ = π/2 (odd kernel); pass 0 for the even one. `ksize` should cover about ±3σ.
- **Filtering**: `cv2.filter2D(img, cv2.CV_32F, kernel)`, float output (responses are signed).
- **Energy**: |response| or response² smoothed with `cv2.blur` / `GaussianBlur`; or combine even and odd kernels: √(even² + odd²) (a phase-independent amplitude).
- **Speed**: a bank of k filters costs k convolutions; for large kernels filter in the frequency domain (30.5).

## Where it fails

- **Wrong scale**: a bank tuned to λ = 8 misses textures of period 20; use several λ (octaves).
- **Irregular textures** (stone, foam): no dominant frequency; statistical descriptors (LBP, GLCM, 35.x) work better.
- **Lighting changes**: energy scales with contrast; normalise by local contrast.
- **Border effects** and the energy window blur the defect outline; refine with morphology.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">

- **Degrees instead of radians** for θ and ψ.
- **Forgetting ψ's default** of π/2 when an even kernel was intended.
- **uint8 filter output**: negative responses are clipped; use CV_32F.
- **Mixing up θ**: θ is the direction across the stripes (θ = 0 detects vertical stripes).

</Callout>

## 🔍 Go deeper

<GoDeeper>

### Uncertainty and optimality

Gabor showed that his elementary signals (Gaussian-windowed waves) reach the lower bound of the joint uncertainty in time and frequency; Daugman [2] extended this to 2-D, where Gabor functions are optimally localised in position and spatial frequency together, and matched them to receptive fields of simple cells in the visual cortex.

### Complex Gabor and log-Gabor

A complex Gabor kernel (even + i · odd) gives amplitude (texture strength) and phase (position of stripes) separately; phase is used for fingerprint and iris codes. Log-Gabor filters (Gaussian on a log-frequency axis) have no DC response and better cover high frequencies, which avoids the mean-brightness issue of the even Gabor.

</GoDeeper>

## Exercises

### Exercise 1 (by hand)

With θ = 0, λ = 8, σ = 4, γ = 0.5, ψ = 0, compute the kernel at (2, 0) and (4, 4).

<Solution>

(2, 0): e^(−4/32) · cos(π/2) = 0.882 · 0 = **0**. (4, 4): e^(−(16 + 4)/32) · cos π = e^(−0.625) · (−1) = **−0.535**.

</Solution>

### Exercise 2 (coding)

Run the 0° filter with λ = 6 and λ = 12 instead of 8. How does the energy outside the band change?

<Solution>

```python
for lam in (6, 8, 12):
    k = cv2.getGaborKernel((21, 21), 4.0, 0, float(lam), 0.5, 0, ktype=cv2.CV_32F)
    e = cv2.blur(np.abs(cv2.filter2D(img - img.mean(), cv2.CV_32F, k)), (15, 15))
```

```text
lambda 8 energy outside the band: 1924.8
```
